Adults need between seven and nine hours of sleep per night, but the quality of sleep matters as much as the quantity. During the night the brain cycles through light sleep, deep sleep and REM sleep several times, and each phase plays a different role in memory and physical recovery.

Going to bed and waking up at the same time every day, even at weekends, is the most effective habit for improving sleep. Bright screens in the hour before bed delay the release of melatonin, the hormone that tells the body it is time to rest, so reading a paper book is a better choice.

Caffeine stays in the body for many hours: a coffee in the late afternoon can still reduce deep sleep at midnight. Alcohol may help you fall asleep faster, but it fragments sleep in the second half of the night and leaves you more tired in the morning.

If you regularly snore loudly, wake up gasping or feel exhausted despite sleeping enough, talk to a doctor: sleep apnea is common and treatable.
