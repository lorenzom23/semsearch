Bats are the only mammals capable of true sustained flight. Their wings are made of a thin membrane of skin stretched between very long finger bones, which gives them remarkable control in the air. Most species are active at night and spend the day hanging upside down in caves, hollow trees or the roofs of old buildings.

Many small bats find their food using echolocation. They emit high pitched calls and listen to the echoes that bounce back from objects around them. From these echoes a bat can judge the distance, size and even the texture of an insect, and catch it in complete darkness. A single brown bat can eat hundreds of mosquitoes in one hour.

Fruit bats, also called flying foxes, rely more on sight and smell. They feed on ripe fruit and nectar, and in doing so they spread seeds and pollinate plants such as bananas, mangoes and agave. Entire tropical forests depend on them to regenerate after storms or fires.

Bats are threatened by habitat loss, pesticides and a fungal disease called white nose syndrome, which has killed millions of them in North America during hibernation. Protecting caves and old trees is one of the simplest ways to help local populations survive the winter.
